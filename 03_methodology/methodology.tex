\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, our methodology unifies circuit representation learning by reformulating the attributed netlist into an evolving point set, progressing through five coordinated stages from graph formulation to unified readout.

\subsection{Point-Set Modality Alignment}
\label{sec:method_alignment}

A digital circuit netlist is naturally represented as an attributed directed graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$, where $\mathcal{V}$ denotes the set of circuit entities (logic gates, primary inputs, and primary outputs) and $\mathcal{E}$ represents directed physical interconnections and causal dependencies (Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, Stage 1). Each node $i \in \mathcal{V}$ is endowed with an attribute vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$ encoding intrinsic properties. Crucially, to prevent dominant discrete categories from overshadowing continuous electrical quantities, $\mathbf{x}_i$ is organized into balanced, disentangled physical channels: functional cell categories, base logic types, continuous drive-strength scalings ($W/L$ ratios), pin directions, and localized fan-out/fan-in impedances. Unlike abstract graphs, each directed relation $(j, i) \in \mathcal{E}$ from driver $j$ to sink $i$ carries a complete multi-dimensional physical feature vector $\mathbf{e}_{ji} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_e}$, decoupling topological edge categories from continuous electrical attributes such as fan-out load capacitance ($C_{\text{load}}$), driver pin impedance, and routing parasitic resistance.

A primary challenge in circuit modeling stems from the representational disparity between these two distinct modalities: nodes exist as individual vectors, whereas relations exist as pair-wise feature vectors. Rather than compressing relation vectors into scalar adjacency weights or constructing hand-crafted heterogeneous graph rules, our framework establishes a unified point-set representation (Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, Stage 2).

Specifically, we view graph modeling as computing a latent structural displacement over the coordinates of an unordered point set $\mathcal{S}^{(0)} = \{\mathbf{s}_1^{(0)}, \dots, \mathbf{s}_{|\mathcal{V}|}^{(0)}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$. For each entity $i \in \mathcal{V}$, an initial feature encoder maps its isolated attributes into a native latent coordinate:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s}_i^{(0)} = f_{\text{node}}(\mathbf{x}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{d},
    \label{eq:node_init}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\text{node}}: \mathbb{R}^{d_v} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ is parameterized by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). Concurrently, a relation encoder projects multi-dimensional relation features into a dense relational space:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{r}_{ji} = f_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf{e}_{ji}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_r},
    \label{eq:rel_init}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\text{rel}}: \mathbb{R}^{d_e} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_r}$ preserves the complete continuous physical attributes without scalar down-scaling. In this aligned formulation, every entity $i$ in the circuit expresses its terminal state $\mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}$ as its native coordinate $\mathbf{s}_i^{(0)}$ displaced by an accumulated structural offset $\boldsymbol{\delta}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)} = \mathbf{s}_i^{(0)} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_i.
    \label{eq:structural_offset}
\end{equation}
By reformulating the circuit graph as a point set characterized by latent structural offsets, we transform the heterogeneous message-passing problem into a homogeneous set-evolution task.

\subsection{Trainable Pairwise Interaction Dynamics Field}
\label{sec:method_dynamics}

To compute the structural displacement $\boldsymbol{\delta}_i$, we construct an end-to-end Trainable Interaction Dynamics Field (Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, Stage 3). Rather than relying on passive neighborhood diffusion, the trajectory of each particle is actively driven by directional pairwise interactions and competitive acquisition coefficients.

As detailed in the microscopic architecture of Figure~\ref{fig:pairwise_dynamics}, for each directed branch $(j, i) \in \mathcal{E}$ where driver $j$ interacts with sink $i$ at layer $l$, we first extract pairwise algebraic divergence features through an operator $\Phi$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{ji}^{(l)} = \Phi\left(\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}\right) = \Big[\, \big(\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)} - \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}\big) \;\|\; \big\|\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)} - \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}\big\|_2 \,\Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{d + 1},
    \label{eq:algebraic_operator}
\end{equation}
which explicitly evaluates the relative displacement vector and Euclidean separation between particles in the latent space. Combining the particle coordinates, algebraic divergence, and continuous relation embeddings yields a comprehensive geometric-relational interaction context:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{evol}}\!\left(\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{r}_{ji}\right) = \Big[\, \mathbf{s}_j^{(l)} \;\|\; \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)} \;\|\; \mathbf{p}_{ji}^{(l)} \;\|\; \mathbf{r}_{ji} \,\Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{3d + 1 + d_r},
    \label{eq:pairwise_basis}
\end{equation}
where $\|$ denotes vector concatenation. A trainable dynamics operator $\Psi: \mathbb{R}^{3d + 1 + d_r} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ maps this context into an all-directional velocity displacement vector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{m}_{ji}^{(l)} = \Psi\!\left(\Phi_{\text{evol}}\!\left(\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{r}_{ji}\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d.
    \label{eq:pairwise_interaction}
\end{equation}
Crucially, the relation vector $\mathbf{r}_{ji}$ participates directly as a continuous physical condition within $\Phi_{\text{evol}}$, allowing attributes such as load capacitance and wire resistance to modulate both the orientation and magnitude of $\mathbf{m}_{ji}^{(l)}$ without scalar compression.

In digital circuits, a sink pin frequently receives signals from multiple driving paths or shares a net with multiple fan-in branches. To model this physical arbitration without imposing static timing analysis traversals, incoming interaction vectors from in-neighbors $j \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)$ undergo localized sibling competition. The competitive driving priority logit is scored from the interaction context, and competitive acquisition coefficients are normalized via localized Softmax:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{ji}^{(l)} = \frac{\exp\!\left(g\big(\Phi_{\text{evol}}(\mathbf{s}_j^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{r}_{ji})\big)\right)}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)} \exp\!\left(g\big(\Phi_{\text{evol}}(\mathbf{s}_k^{(l)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}, \mathbf{r}_{ki})\big)\right)},
    \label{eq:sibling_competition}
\end{equation}
where $g: \mathbb{R}^{3d + 1 + d_r} \to \mathbb{R}$ evaluates the relative competitive strength of branch $(j, i)$. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:pairwise_dynamics}, the net velocity displacement vector acting on particle $i$ at layer $l$ is aggregated by weighting candidate displacements with acquisition coefficients:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i)} \alpha_{ji}^{(l)} \mathbf{m}_{ji}^{(l)}.
    \label{eq:displacement_agg}
\end{equation}
Because $\Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}$ is an all-directional displacement vector rather than a convex combination of neighbor states, it acts as an active force vector updating the latent coordinate of particle $i$ from $\mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}$ to $\mathbf{s}_i^{(l+1)}$. Notably, for root entities with zero in-degree such as primary inputs and clock sources ($\mathcal{N}_{\text{in}}(i) = \emptyset$), the empty-set summation naturally evaluates to $\Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)} = \mathbf{0}$, preserving zero-displacement boundary invariance without requiring ad-hoc structural constraints.

\subsection{Deep Multi-Layer Evolution}
\label{sec:method_evolution}

With the displacement vector $\Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}$ established, each particle updates its latent coordinate through a continuous additive step:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{s}_i^{(l+1)} = \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)} + \Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}.
    \label{eq:layer_update}
\end{equation}
Over $L$ stacked interaction layers (Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, Stage 4), the cumulative structural offset is the integral sum of these directional displacements:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\delta}_i = \sum_{l=0}^{L-1} \Delta \mathbf{s}_i^{(l)}.
    \label{eq:accumulated_delta}
\end{equation}

This multi-layer evolution exhibits distinct progressive dynamics across depths. In the initial layers, the interaction field induces larger structural displacements, reorganizing the point set to encode macro-connectivity and global topological distances. As the network deepens across dozens of layers, the displacement magnitudes adaptively decrease, executing fine-grained non-linear adjustments to align timing arrivals and local driving balances.

Importantly, this formulation maintains representation discriminability throughout deep propagation. In conventional message passing, repeated Laplacian averaging contracts node embeddings into the convex hull of their neighbors, collapsing distinct representations toward a uniform stationary mean. In contrast, our pairwise dynamics computes unconstrained, all-directional velocity offsets modulated by competitive acquisition coefficients and multi-dimensional physical relations. This mechanism preserves high-frequency feature variance across dozens of layers, enabling the model to capture deep multi-hop causal chains without suffering from feature collapse.

\subsection{Prior-Free Intrinsic Readout via Virtual Meter Nodes}
\label{sec:method_readout}

Through $L$ layers of interaction evolution, explicit topological and physical edge priors $\mathbf{r}_{ji}$ are progressively internalized into the geometric configuration of the terminal point set $\mathcal{S}^{(L)} = \{\mathbf{s}_1^{(L)}, \dots, \mathbf{s}_{|\mathcal{V}|}^{(L)}\}$. Consequently, downstream circuit predictions are decoded directly from the intrinsic geometry of $\mathcal{S}^{(L)}$ without re-injecting explicit edge priors or requiring sequential graph post-processing (Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework}, Stage 5).

\textbf{Node-Level and Pair-Level Intrinsic Readout.} For micro-level predictions associated with individual pins or gates (such as pin arrival time, slew, or local cell leakage), predictions are read out directly from terminal particle coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_i = \text{MLP}_{\text{node}}\left(\mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}\right).
    \label{eq:readout_node}
\end{equation}
For relation-level tasks defined over interconnections or arbitrary entity pairs (such as net wirelength or interconnect delay), we strip away external edge priors $\mathbf{r}_{ji}$ and construct an isomorphic pairwise geometric basis purely from terminal particle coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\text{read}}\left(\mathbf{s}_j^{(L)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}\right) = \Big[\, \mathbf{s}_j^{(L)} \;\|\; \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)} \;\|\; \big(\mathbf{s}_j^{(L)} - \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}\big) \;\|\; \big\|\mathbf{s}_j^{(L)} - \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}\big\|_2 \,\Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{3d + 1},
    \label{eq:readout_basis}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{ji} = \text{MLP}_{\text{pair}}\left(\Phi_{\text{read}}\left(\mathbf{s}_j^{(L)}, \mathbf{s}_i^{(L)}\right)\right).
    \label{eq:readout_pair}
\end{equation}
Because relational physics have been encoded into relative particle displacements during evolution, this prior-free interface evaluates any connected or unconnected node pair in $O(1)$ complexity per query.

\textbf{Whole-Graph Readout via Virtual Meter Nodes.} Extracting whole-circuit metrics, such as total placement area or total dynamic power, conventionally requires global pooling operators (e.g., sum, mean, or attention pooling). However, flat pooling over thousands of nodes frequently dilutes critical localized bottlenecks.

To overcome this limitation, we introduce a hierarchical virtual meter network, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:virtual_meter}. Specifically, we insert dedicated virtual meter nodes, denoted as $v_{\text{meter}} \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{virt}}$, into the circuit graph. Structured in a multi-level reduction pyramid, intermediate meters condense regional circuit clusters while a top-level root meter aggregates global status. Assigned initial measurement query vectors $\mathbf{x}_{\text{meter}}$ and connected to constituent circuit nodes through specialized virtual relation edges $\mathbf{e}_{\text{meter}}$, these virtual entities participate directly in the point-set interaction dynamics alongside regular circuit nodes under Equations~\eqref{eq:algebraic_operator}--\eqref{eq:layer_update}. Whole-graph metrics $\hat{Y}$ are then obtained by reading out the evolved root meter coordinate identically to standard physical nodes:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{Y} = \text{MLP}_{\text{meter}}\left(\mathbf{s}_{\text{meter}}^{(L)}\right).
    \label{eq:readout_graph}
\end{equation}
This formulation unifies whole-graph metric extraction into the exact same point-set readout interface, establishing a clean, end-to-end framework without task-specific graph post-processing.
