\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The advent of large language models (LLMs) is fundamentally transforming integrated circuit (IC) design, enabling automated and rapid generation of hardware description code from high-level functional specifications. However, realizing the promise of software-like agile hardware development—characterized by continuous, push-button design iterations—critically hinges on accelerating the physical evaluation loop, which currently represents the primary bottleneck. While generative models can produce register-transfer level (RTL) code almost instantaneously, evaluating circuit properties still requires prolonged turn-around times to run synthesis, placement, and timing analysis. To bridge this disparity, foundational circuit representation learning paradigms that can accurately and efficiently capture post-implementation characteristics are essential. Integrated circuits are naturally formulated as graphs, where logic cells and physical interconnections constitute nodes and edges endowed with rich and heterogeneous attributes, ranging from cell driving strengths to multi-dimensional routing parasitics. Moreover, predicting circuit characteristics inherently demands capturing long-range, multi-hop propagation dynamics across deep topological and causal logic chains. Consequently, pioneering dedicated graph learning algorithms specifically engineered to handle complex node-edge representations while scaling effectively across deep multi-hop circuit paths is of paramount theoretical and practical importance.

To model physical timing, signal dynamic switching, and routing propagation behaviors, circuit graph learning fundamentally requires capturing multi-hop topological dependencies. Under the conventional message-passing paradigm~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velickovic2018gat}, gathering multi-hop structural context necessitates stacking a corresponding number of message-passing layers. In realistic circuits, causal logic chains and signal paths routinely span dozens to hundreds of topological levels, requiring dozens of sequential message-passing steps to trace these multi-hop dependencies. However, deep message-passing architectures inevitably suffer from the fundamental limitation of over-smoothing~\cite{rusch2023survey}, where recursive Laplacian neighborhood aggregation drives node embeddings toward uniform representations, extinguishing subtle differences between logic paths and ultimately causing modeling failures. Furthermore, circuit interconnections encapsulate rich physical attributes that extend far beyond simple adjacency: nodes are subject to physical fan-out load capacitances, driving pin impedances, and multi-dimensional routing parasitic RC relationships. Most existing circuit graph algorithms compress such multi-dimensional edge features into scalar weights~\cite{velickovic2018gat,preroutgnn} or recast distinct features into heterogeneous graph formulations that demand cumbersome heuristic pre-processing and post-processing~\cite{timingpredict,deepgate2}. Such treatments not only induce severe physical information loss during the modeling process, but also compromise the algorithmic generality across diverse EDA downstream stages.

Our key insight posits that graph modeling is fundamentally a process of computing latent structural displacement over the embeddings. In this view, a node's native feature vector—obtained in isolation via its initial encoder—undergoes a structural offset when embedded within the complex topology of a circuit. To resolve the representational gap between disparate modalities, where nodes are individual vectors and edges are pair-wise vectors, we formulate a unified point-set representation. Rather than treating edges as external topological constraints or collapsed scalar weights, we map both nodes and relations into a unified latent space as a cohesive point set characterized by structural offsets. In this framework, every node expresses its embedding as a synthesis of its native properties and the accumulated structural displacement induced by graph topology. By reinterpreting the graph as a point set, we transform the circuit modeling problem from a heterogeneous message-passing challenge into a homogeneous set-modeling task, enabling seamless alignment of node and edge information through their collective contributions to latent space displacement.

We instantiate this conceptual framework through an end-to-end Trainable Interaction Dynamics Field. Departing from passive neighborhood aggregation, our approach models point-set evolution by explicitly learning pairwise interaction patterns. We feed element-wise operations between intermediate node embeddings into a learnable dynamics function, which outputs all-directional displacement vectors in the latent space. This establishes an adaptive interaction field that dynamically modulates the state of each node based on the underlying graph topology. As nodes evolve across dozens of interaction layers, the field performs continuous non-linear refinement, allowing for large structural displacements in initial layers to capture macro-connectivity and fine-grained adjustments in deeper layers to converge on high-fidelity features. Unlike traditional message passing that collapses representations toward Laplacian uniformities, our dynamics field maintains the discriminability of node embeddings across arbitrary depths by ensuring that structural evolution is driven by directed, element-wise interactions rather than indiscriminate averaging. This allows our framework to achieve extreme modeling depth without the threat of over-smoothing, forming an end-to-end differentiable pipeline that seamlessly fuses topological structure with rich multi-dimensional physical attributes.
