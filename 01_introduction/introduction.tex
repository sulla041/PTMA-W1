\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rapid development of large language models (LLMs) is transforming integrated circuit (IC) design, enabling automated and fast generation of hardware description code from high-level specifications. However, realizing software-like agile hardware development critically depends on accelerating physical evaluation, which is currently the main bottleneck. While generative models produce register-transfer level (RTL) code in seconds, evaluating circuit properties still requires hours or days for synthesis, placement, and timing analysis. To bridge this gap, foundational circuit representation learning methods that can accurately and efficiently capture post-implementation properties are essential. Integrated circuits are naturally represented as graphs, where logic cells and interconnects form nodes and edges with rich attributes, such as cell driving strengths and multi-dimensional routing parasitics. Moreover, predicting circuit properties requires capturing long-range, multi-hop propagation dynamics along deep logic paths. Therefore, designing dedicated graph learning algorithms that handle complex node-edge representations while scaling across deep multi-hop paths is of vital importance.

To model physical timing, signal dynamic switching, and routing propagation behaviors, circuit graph learning must capture multi-hop dependencies across deep logic networks, as illustrated in the top panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. Under the conventional message-passing neural network (MPNN) paradigm~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velickovic2018gat}, gathering multi-hop context requires stacking a matching number of message-passing layers. In realistic circuits, signal paths frequently span dozens of topological levels, requiring dozens of sequential message-passing steps. However, deep message passing inevitably suffers from over-smoothing~\cite{rusch2023survey}, as depicted in the middle panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. Mathematically, recursive message passing functions as a continuous Laplacian smoothing operator: at each layer, node embeddings are updated by averaging their immediate neighbors. As the network depth grows to dozens of hops, this repeated spatial averaging contracts node features, driving distinct embeddings toward an undifferentiated stationary mean. In circuit modeling, this mathematical collapse washes out the distinct functional identities of logic gates as well as the arrival-time differences accumulated along propagation paths, leading to modeling failure. Furthermore, circuit interconnections encapsulate rich physical attributes beyond simple topological adjacency, including driver impedances, fan-out load capacitances ($C_{\text{load}}$), and multi-dimensional routing parasitic RC networks. Most existing circuit graph algorithms either compress these multi-dimensional edge vectors into scalar weights~\cite{velickovic2018gat,preroutgnn}, or construct complex heterogeneous graphs requiring heuristic pre-processing and post-processing~\cite{timingpredict,deepgate2}. These treatments cause severe physical information loss and limit algorithmic generality across diverse EDA stages.

Our key insight is that graph modeling is fundamentally a process of computing latent structural displacement over node embeddings. A node's native feature vector, obtained in isolation from an initial encoder, undergoes a structural offset when placed inside a circuit topology. To bridge the gap between individual node vectors and pair-wise edge vectors, we formulate a unified point-set representation. Rather than treating edges as external constraints or compressed scalar weights, we map both nodes and relations into a unified latent space as a point set with structural offsets, as shown in the bottom panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. In this framework, every node expresses its embedding as a combination of its native properties and the accumulated structural displacement induced by graph topology. By treating the graph as a point set, we convert the heterogeneous message-passing problem into a unified set-modeling task, aligning node and edge information through their collective contributions to latent space displacement.

We instantiate this framework through an end-to-end Trainable Interaction Dynamics Field. Departing from passive neighborhood averaging, our approach models point-set evolution by explicitly learning pairwise interaction patterns. We feed element-wise operations of intermediate node embeddings together with complete multi-dimensional relation feature vectors into a learnable dynamics function, which produces all-directional displacement vectors in the latent space. This forms an adaptive interaction field that dynamically updates each node state according to the circuit topology. This interaction field avoids over-smoothing across dozens of layers because it is non-dissipative: instead of computing convex combinations of neighbor states, our dynamics computes directional velocity offsets governed by learned pairwise operators and explicit sibling competition. As nodes evolve across dozens of interaction layers, the field performs continuous non-linear refinement, allowing large structural displacements in initial layers to capture macro-connectivity and fine adjustments in deeper layers to converge on high-fidelity features, all while preserving the discriminability of node embeddings. Furthermore, to capture whole-graph properties within this unified framework, we introduce a virtual-node graph information aggregation scheme. Specifically, measurement-specific virtual nodes are connected to the circuit graph as a specialized node type, with their incident edges marked as a specialized relation type. Both the virtual nodes and their associated edges are assigned corresponding feature vectors, naturally participating in the graph-to-point-set dynamic embedding process alongside regular nodes. Downstream tasks, ranging from fine-grained pin characteristics to whole-graph metrics, are then accomplished directly by reading out the resulting embeddings.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a trainable interaction dynamics field model that maps the circuit graph into a point set and learns structural embeddings through pairwise interaction patterns, preventing over-smoothing across dozens of layers while enabling multi-dimensional relation features to participate directly in the dynamics.
    \item We propose a virtual-node graph information aggregation scheme, which unifies the extraction of whole-graph properties into the standard point-set embedding readout problem.
    \item We conduct extensive experiments across diverse downstream tasks on the open-source CircuitNet-3 and DeepGate benchmarks, validating the effectiveness and algorithmic generality of our proposed methodology.
\end{itemize}
