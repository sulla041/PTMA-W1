\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rapid development of large language models (LLMs) is transforming integrated circuit (IC) design, enabling automated and fast generation of hardware description code from high-level specifications. However, realizing software-like agile hardware development critically depends on accelerating physical evaluation, which is currently the main bottleneck. While generative models produce register-transfer level (RTL) code in seconds, evaluating circuit properties still requires hours or days for synthesis, placement, and timing analysis. To bridge this gap, foundational circuit representation learning methods that can accurately and efficiently capture post-implementation properties are essential. Integrated circuits are naturally represented as graphs, where logic cells and interconnects form nodes and edges with rich attributes, such as cell driving strengths and multi-dimensional routing parasitics. Moreover, predicting circuit properties requires capturing long-range, multi-hop propagation dynamics along deep logic paths. Therefore, designing dedicated graph learning algorithms that handle complex node-edge representations while scaling across deep multi-hop paths is of vital importance.

To model physical timing, signal dynamic switching, and routing propagation behaviors, circuit graph learning must capture multi-hop dependencies across deep logic networks, as illustrated in the top panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. Under the conventional message-passing neural network (MPNN) paradigm~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velickovic2018gat}, gathering multi-hop context requires stacking a matching number of message-passing layers. In realistic circuits, signal paths frequently span dozens of topological levels, requiring dozens of sequential message-passing steps. However, deep message passing inevitably suffers from over-smoothing~\cite{rusch2023survey}, as depicted in the middle panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. Mathematically, recursive message passing functions as a continuous Laplacian smoothing operator: at each layer, node embeddings are updated by averaging their immediate neighbors. As the network depth grows to dozens of hops, this repeated spatial averaging contracts node features, driving distinct embeddings toward an undifferentiated stationary mean. In circuit modeling, this mathematical collapse washes out the distinct functional identities of logic gates as well as the arrival-time differences accumulated along propagation paths, leading to modeling failure. Furthermore, circuit interconnections encapsulate rich physical attributes beyond simple topological adjacency, including driver impedances, fan-out load capacitances ($C_{\text{load}}$), and multi-dimensional routing parasitic RC networks. Most existing circuit graph algorithms either compress these multi-dimensional edge vectors into scalar weights~\cite{velickovic2018gat,preroutgnn}, or construct complex heterogeneous graphs requiring heuristic pre-processing and post-processing~\cite{timingpredict,deepgate2}. These treatments cause severe physical information loss and limit algorithmic generality across diverse EDA stages.

\textbf{Our key insight is that graph modeling is fundamentally a process of computing latent structural displacement over the coordinates of an evolving point set.} A circuit entity's native feature vector, obtained in isolation from an initial encoder, defines its initial latent coordinate, which undergoes structural displacements when situated within a circuit topology. Rather than treating edges as external constraints or compressed scalar weights, we project both nodes and relations into a unified latent space as a point set $\mathcal{S} = \{\mathbf{s}_1, \dots, \mathbf{s}_{|\mathcal{V}|}\}$ governed by structural offsets, as shown in the bottom panel of Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}. In this framework, every entity expresses its final representation as its native coordinate displaced by the accumulated structural offset induced by topological and electrical interactions. By treating the graph as a point set, we convert the heterogeneous message-passing problem into a unified set-evolution task, aligning entity and relation information through their collective contributions to latent space displacement.

\textbf{Under this formulation, we propose that the core of set modeling lies in modeling the interaction patterns among the constituent elements.} We instantiate this framework through an end-to-end Trainable Interaction Dynamics Field. Departing from passive neighborhood averaging, our approach models point-set evolution by explicitly learning pairwise interaction patterns. We feed algebraic divergence features of intermediate particle coordinates together with complete multi-dimensional relation feature vectors into a learnable dynamics function, which produces all-directional velocity displacement vectors in the latent space. This forms an adaptive interaction field that dynamically updates each particle coordinate according to circuit topology. Unlike traditional message passing that performs spatial convex combinations, our dynamics computes directional velocity offsets governed by learned pairwise operators and competitive acquisition coefficients. This mechanism preserves high-frequency variance in particle representations across dozens of layers, preventing the feature collapse commonly associated with deep neighborhood averaging. As particles evolve across dozens of interaction layers, the field performs continuous non-linear refinement, allowing large structural displacements in initial layers to capture macro-connectivity and fine adjustments in deeper layers to converge on high-fidelity features, all while maintaining the discriminability of representations. Furthermore, to capture whole-graph properties within this unified framework, we introduce a hierarchical virtual meter network. Specifically, specialized virtual meter nodes are connected into the circuit graph in a multi-level reduction pyramid, co-evolving alongside physical entities. Downstream tasks, ranging from fine-grained pin characteristics to whole-graph metrics, are then accomplished directly by reading out the resulting point-set coordinates.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a trainable interaction dynamics field model that maps the circuit graph into a point set and learns structural representations through pairwise interaction patterns and competitive acquisition coefficients, overcoming over-smoothing across dozens of layers via directional displacement updates, while enabling multi-dimensional relation features to participate directly in the dynamics.
    \item We propose a hierarchical virtual meter network, which unifies the extraction of macroscopic whole-graph metrics into a standard single-node point-set readout interface.
    \item We conduct extensive experiments across diverse downstream tasks on the open-source CircuitNet-3 and DeepGate benchmarks, validating the effectiveness and algorithmic generality of our proposed methodology.
\end{itemize}
