\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The rapid emergence of large language models (LLMs) for automated register-transfer level (RTL) generation accelerates early chip design, yet realizing software-like agile development critically hinges on rapid physical evaluation. While generative models produce RTL code in seconds, evaluating physical timing and power via downstream synthesis and layout still requires hours or days. Fast and accurate circuit graph representation learning is therefore vital to bridge this gap. However, digital circuits present a unique modeling dilemma: signal propagation traverses deep multi-hop logic chains, while interconnections carry multi-dimensional continuous physical parasitics, such as driving impedances and fan-out load capacitances ($C_{\text{load}}$).

Conventional message-passing neural networks (MPNNs)~\cite{kipf2017gcn,velickovic2018gat} fail to address both challenges simultaneously (Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}). First, capturing deep logic dependencies requires stacking dozens of layers, where recursive neighborhood averaging acts as a continuous Laplacian smoothing operator, collapsing distinct gate identities and timing differentials into uniform states via over-smoothing~\cite{rusch2023survey}. Second, existing circuit GNNs typically compress multi-dimensional interconnection parasitics into scalar adjacency weights~\cite{velickovic2018gat,preroutgnn} or rely on ad-hoc heterogeneous graph heuristics~\cite{timingpredict,deepgate2}, severely discarding continuous electrical context.

\textbf{Our key insight is that graph modeling is fundamentally a process of computing latent structural displacements over an aligned set of circuit elements.} Rather than treating logic cells and interconnections as disparate modalities requiring ad-hoc routing heuristics or lossy scalar compression, we project the entire circuit graph into a unified latent space as a point set characterized by structural offsets (Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm}, bottom). In this framework, every circuit entity is initialized in isolation and subsequently undergoes continuous coordinate displacement induced by the collective topological and physical dynamics. By aligning the heterogeneous graph into a unified set of elements, we transform the message-passing problem into a homogeneous set-evolution task, where structural topology is directly internalized as latent space displacements.

\textbf{Under this formulation, we propose that the core of set modeling lies in transforming edge-dependent message passing into a comprehensive dynamic interaction governed by node states, algebraic operations, and multi-dimensional relations.} Conventional GNNs strictly constrain information flow to explicit edges and spatial neighborhood smoothing. However, in physical circuits, explicit topological wires represent merely one category of relations: physical behavior equally encompasses non-connected dependencies such as parasitic cross-coupling, and even the absence of an edge inherently constitutes an informative relationship. In contrast, our framework models structural evolution through an end-to-end Trainable Interaction Dynamics Field. In this field, the interaction between elements is no longer a passive edge traversal, but is jointly determined by the interacting node states, their pairwise algebraic operations (explicitly evaluating relative coordinate differences and latent separations), and continuous multi-dimensional physical relations (such as load capacitances and routing impedances). To arbitrate competitive signals from multiple fan-in driving paths, we introduce localized sibling competition, where competitive acquisition coefficients weight incoming velocity pushes into a net structural offset. Because updates are formulated as directed force vectors rather than spatial convex combinations, this mechanism naturally preserves high-frequency feature variance across dozens of layers, preventing the over-smoothing collapse of conventional GNNs. Furthermore, as relational physics are progressively internalized into the terminal geometry, downstream predictions are decoded via prior-free intrinsic readout. To capture whole-chip properties, a hierarchical virtual meter network co-evolves alongside physical entities in a reduction pyramid, unifying macroscopic graph prediction into standard single-element readout.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a trainable interaction dynamics field that aligns the circuit graph into an evolving point set, driving structural displacements through pairwise algebraic operations and competitive acquisition coefficients while overcoming over-smoothing across deep propagation.
    \item We propose a hierarchical virtual meter network structured in a reduction pyramid, which co-evolves with circuit entities to unify macroscopic whole-graph prediction into the same point-set readout interface.
    \item We conduct extensive experiments across diverse downstream tasks on the open-source CircuitNet-3 and DeepGate2 benchmarks, validating the algorithmic generality and superior accuracy of our proposed methodology.
\end{itemize}
