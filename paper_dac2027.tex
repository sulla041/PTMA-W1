\title{Dynamic Interactions Define the Embedding: End-to-End Point-Set Graph Modeling for Circuit Feature Prediction}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Integrated circuits are naturally abstracted as graphs, where circuit components are represented by node feature vectors and interconnections are captured by edges. Consequently, graph modeling algorithms have become a prominent paradigm for circuit analysis and modeling, with graph neural networks (GNNs) demonstrating significant potential in prior work. However, conventional GNNs fundamentally rely on message passing algorithms; when modeling multi-hop information across deep paths, message passing inherently suffers from over-smoothing, leading to modeling failure. Furthermore, physical interconnections carry critical attributes such as load capacitances, and placed components exhibit complex parasitic resistances and geometric relationships in real routing scenarios. Treating interconnections merely as topological edges inevitably loses circuit information, while existing graph algorithms typically compress edge feature vectors into scalar weights, exacerbating information loss. To address these two challenges, we propose a novel framework that maps the circuit graph into a point set and embeds the graph by training pairwise interaction patterns between point-set elements. Trainable pairwise interactions naturally resolve the over-smoothing dilemma, while directly incorporating complete multi-dimensional relation feature vectors into the interaction process to preserve rich physical information. Downstream tasks are subsequently accomplished by reading out node embeddings and pairwise interactions. Simultaneously, we introduce an information aggregation scheme based on inserted virtual nodes, unifying whole-graph metric extraction into the same embedding readout formulation. Comprehensive experiments across multiple downstream tasks on open-source benchmarks (CircuitNet and DeepGate) demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed methodology.
\end{abstract}
