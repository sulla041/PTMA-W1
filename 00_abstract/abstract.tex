Integrated circuits are naturally abstracted as graphs, making graph modeling a prominent paradigm for circuit analysis and modeling. However, conventional graph neural networks fundamentally rely on message passing that suffers from over-smoothing across deep multi-hop paths, while most existing graph methods compress multi-dimensional interconnections into scalar weights or fail to preserve fine-grained parasitic attributes, causing severe loss of physical parasitics and load characteristics. To address these challenges, we propose an end-to-end point-set graph dynamics framework that maps the circuit graph into a point set and learns embeddings by training pairwise interaction patterns. Crucially, the learned adaptive interaction patterns support evolution across substantially deeper layers without suffering from over-smoothing, while enabling complete multi-dimensional relation feature vectors to participate directly in the interaction dynamics. Downstream tasks are performed by reading out node embeddings and pairwise interactions. Furthermore, inserted virtual nodes unify whole-graph metric extraction into the same embedding readout formulation. Extensive experiments across diverse downstream tasks on open-source benchmarks (CircuitNet and DeepGate) validate the effectiveness of our proposed methodology.
